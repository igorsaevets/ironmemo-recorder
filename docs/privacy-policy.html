Privacy Policy — IronMemo Audio Recorder

Effective date: 21 September 2026 · Applies to extension v0.6.0 and later · Back to home

IronMemo Audio Recorder ("the extension") is a Chrome extension that records microphone and browser tab audio on the user's own device. Starting with v0.6.0, the extension can also send one recording at a time to IronMemo servers for transcription — always after a separate consent dialog and never automatically. This policy explains exactly what data the extension handles, what is optional, and what happens on the server if you choose to use it.

Summary. Audio recording is local. Transcription is optional and only starts when you click Transcribe with IronMemo on a specific recording. Nothing else is uploaded. IronMemo does not train models on your recordings.

1. Data the extension handles

  • Microphone audio — captured only during a recording you have explicitly started. Chrome Web Store category: Personal communications.
  • Browser tab audio — captured only during a recording you have explicitly started, and only from the specific tab you chose. Category: Website content.
  • Extension settings and consent state — small key/value pairs stored in chrome.storage.local: recording preferences, cloud-consent version, timestamps, and (after v0.6.0) IronMemo authentication tokens. Category: Website content.
  • Your e-mail address — only when you enter it in the Add your e-mail dialog. Used as the login of your IronMemo account and to deliver a one-time verification code. Categories: Authentication information and Personally identifiable information.
  • IronMemo authentication tokens — issued by IronMemo servers to your extension after a guest session or e-mail verification. Category: Authentication information.
  • Diagnostic counters — how many errors of each kind occurred in the extension. Kept in chrome.storage.local, never sent anywhere by the extension itself, and only exposed on your clipboard when you press Copy diagnostics. Contain no recording content, no e-mail, no identifiers.
  • Session names and timeline markers — custom labels and time-stamped annotations you create in the session list. Stored locally in the browser's Origin Private File System (OPFS), alongside the recording files. Never uploaded to any server.

The extension does not handle any of the following: web browsing history, form input, passwords, location, health data, financial information, biometric identifiers, personal communications outside of the user-initiated recording, or the content of tabs beyond the audio you chose to capture.

2. Where recordings are stored

All recorded audio and transcript files are written to the browser's Origin Private File System (OPFS) on your device. They stay in OPFS until you delete them. Uninstalling the extension deletes them all. The extension makes no automatic network request to any external service for a recording; the only outbound traffic ever originates from a Transcribe click you made.

3. Optional upload to IronMemo

Each recording carries a Transcribe with IronMemo button. Clicking it shows a separate consent dialog and, if you accept, uploads that one recording to IronMemo servers for transcription. The extension keeps the local copy at the same time — the upload is a copy, not a move.

What is sent

  • The audio file of the recording you chose — either the microphone track, the tab-audio track, or the mix, depending on the recording.
  • The recording's title (its date and time only), source type (dictaphone for mic-only recordings; for tab recordings, the detected platform — Zoom, Google Meet, Teams, or other — derived from the tab's domain, never the URL itself), duration and size.
  • Your e-mail address, only when you enter it in the Add your e-mail dialog. IronMemo delivers a one-time code to it and uses it as the login of your account.
  • A device identifier generated locally by the extension for guest sessions.
  • An X-IronMemo-Client: ironmemo-extension/<version> HTTP header on every request, so IronMemo can distinguish traffic by extension version. No user data is placed in this header.

What is not sent

  • Other recordings — only the one you clicked Transcribe on.
  • Page text, page URLs, browsing history, passwords, form data, or the content of tabs you did not choose to capture.
  • Location, contacts, calendar events, or any operating-system data.

Where it goes

The upload target is app.ironmemo.com. The extension holds this origin as an optional host permission: it is requested from Chrome only when you accept the cloud consent, and no host-permission warning is shown at install time.

IronMemo performs the transcription itself and delegates individual steps to third-party sub-processors. As of the effective date shown at the top of this page:

Speech-to-text

  • ElevenLabs Scribe — used for most languages. Retention policy: elevenlabs.io/docs/eleven-api/resources/zero-retention-mode.
  • AssemblyAI — used as a wide-tail fallback (models best and universal). Retention policy: assemblyai.com/docs/data-retention-and-model-training.
  • GigaAM — used for Russian.

Summaries, action items, AI chat

  • OpenAI, Google, xAI — the transcript text (not the audio) may be sent to one of these providers to generate a summary, extract action items, or answer questions about the recording. Which provider is used depends on the language and the task.
  • DeepInfra — vector embeddings of transcript text, used for search and AI chat. No audio is sent.

The provider used for a given recording is recorded in the transcript metadata (engine.asr_model_id for speech-to-text). The full and current list of sub-processors is maintained at ironmemo.com/subprocessors; if the list changes, this page is updated together with the extension update that changes it.

Retention on the server

  • The audio file is kept on IronMemo so you can request a paid re-processing of a recording that hit the free cap. You can delete the server copy at any time from the extension's Recordings page — the local files stay on your device.
  • Transcripts and summaries are kept on IronMemo under your account until you delete them. Deleting the recording on the server deletes its transcript and summary together.
  • Guest sessions (uploads without an e-mail) expire after 7 days of inactivity. After that the extension can no longer open them. Add your e-mail (the Add your e-mail dialog) to convert a guest session into a durable account before expiry.

No model training

IronMemo does not use your recordings, transcripts, or summaries to train speech-to-text models, language models, or any other AI system. This is a policy commitment of IronMemo the service. The third-party sub-processors above operate under their own terms, linked in this section — check them if that is a decision you need to verify independently.

Cost

The extension is free to install and use for local recording. Transcription of the first 10 minutes is free — per account, granted once your e-mail is verified. Beyond that a recording is processed only if the account has IronMemo credits. There is no subscription and no monthly cap in addition to that one-time 10-minute grant.

4. Retention and deletion — full picture

  • Local recording and transcript files — kept in OPFS until you delete them from the Recordings page. Uninstalling the extension deletes them all.
  • Server-side audio, transcripts, summaries — kept under your IronMemo account until you delete the recording on the server, or delete the account on app.ironmemo.com.
  • Extension settings and tokens — kept in chrome.storage.local. Sign out in the extension wipes the IronMemo tokens; other settings survive. Uninstalling the extension deletes all settings.
  • Diagnostic counters — kept in chrome.storage.local. Never sent anywhere by the extension; exposed on the clipboard only when you press Copy diagnostics. Never contain a recording, an e-mail, or the content of a transcript.

5. Sharing

The extension does not sell, transfer, or share user data for advertising or profile-building. There is no analytics provider, no advertising network, and no crash-reporting service embedded in the extension itself.

When you choose to Transcribe a recording, the audio file and its metadata are shared with IronMemo and the sub-processors listed in section 3: speech-to-text providers receive the audio, and LLM providers receive the transcript text for summaries and AI features. No other third party receives your data.

6. Chrome Web Store Limited Use compliance

The use of information received from Google APIs and handled by this extension adheres to the Chrome Web Store User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements. Data is used solely for the extension's single purpose (audio recording and optional transcription) and is not transferred for advertising, sold to data brokers, or used for creditworthiness assessment.

7. AI use

Local recording performs no AI processing. When you click Transcribe with IronMemo, the audio is converted into a text transcript by the speech-to-text sub-processors named in section 3, and IronMemo may produce a summary, action items, or AI-chat answers from that transcript using the LLM sub-processors also named in section 3. Only the transcript text is sent to LLM providers — not the audio. IronMemo does not train models on your recordings, transcripts, or summaries (see section 3).

8. Recording consent and user responsibility

The extension shows a consent screen in the popup on first use that summarizes what it does and what it does not do. When you first click Transcribe, a second, separate consent dialog explains what is uploaded and where, notes that speech-to-text sub-processors are involved, and links to this privacy policy for the detailed list of providers. You must accept it before any upload starts.

By starting a recording, you confirm that you have the right to record all participants under the law of your jurisdiction. Recording laws differ between countries and, within the United States, between states — the user is responsible for compliance with the law that applies to them.

9. Permissions used, and why

  • tabCapture — required to record audio from the tab you select.
  • offscreen — required by Manifest V3 to hold the recording pipeline while the popup is closed.
  • storage — required to save recording settings, consent state, and IronMemo tokens in chrome.storage.local.
  • unlimitedStorage — required so long recordings are not truncated by Chrome's default quota (about 21.6 MB per hour per track of Opus 48 kbps audio; a meeting recording with all tracks enabled can reach about 65 MB per hour. A full workday exceeds the default quota).
  • optional_host_permissions: ["https://app.ironmemo.com/*"] — requested only when you click Transcribe and accept the cloud consent. Denied or later revoked access means no upload; local recording continues to work.

The extension declares no static host permissions. The full manifest.json is visible in the public repository at github.com/igorsaevets/ironmemo-recorder.

10. Your rights

You can at any time:

  • Delete a recording locally from the Recordings page.
  • Delete the server copy of a recording from the same page. Local files are kept.
  • Sign out of your IronMemo account in the extension. The server copies stay under your account until you delete them; your local files stay on your device.
  • Revoke app.ironmemo.com host permission from Chrome's extensions page. The extension pauses any in-flight upload and starts no new one.
  • Delete your IronMemo account on app.ironmemo.com. That erases the server copies of your recordings, transcripts, and summaries.
  • Uninstall the extension. That deletes all local files, tokens and settings on your device; server copies remain on IronMemo under the same account until deleted separately.
